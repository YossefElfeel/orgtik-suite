# HR benchmark: OrgTik HR vs BambooHR, Personio, Zoho People

**Expected and Missing: 12. Expected and Partial: 15** (Have: 12 of 39 Expected).
**Worthwhile differentiators: 9.** OrgTik already has 2 (loans with instalments, HR helpdesk), 2 are Partial (suite account provisioning, Tasks link) and 5 are Missing. One more is parked for later (field-level permissions) and one item is Not for us (a statutory payroll engine).
The core people, leave and payroll flows exist. The gaps are the employee lifecycle (onboarding tasks, offboarding, assets), leave policy depth, employee self-service, performance, and multi-country money and holidays.

Method: read on 2026-10-07 from `apps/hr` (routes, RTK slices, components), the mock API (`mock/apps/hr.js`, `mock/apps/hr/routes/*.js`, `calc.js`, `seed.js`), `program/audits/hr/wave-plan.md` and `program/audits/inventory/routes.csv`. A feature counts as Have only when a screen uses it. If the mock has an endpoint but no screen calls it, the feature is Partial. Reference products were checked against their public feature pages and reviews. Ratings use these terms:
- **Expected:** an SMB owner assumes the feature exists.
- **Differentiator:** the feature would set OrgTik apart. ★ marks the ones worth building, which suit OrgTik's suite or its Swiss and Middle East customers.
- **Not for us:** the feature is out of scope.

Sizes: S is up to about 3 days, M is 1 to 2 weeks, L is more than 2 weeks, front end plus API.

Paths are relative to `orgtik-suite-v2/`. `hr/` means `apps/hr/`, and `(d)` means `app/[locale]/(dashboard)`.

Observations from reading the code:
- Attendance only works for teams that work in Discord voice channels (#18). For an office or shop owner it shows nothing.
- `FinanceSection.tsx` still shows "CHF" for amounts that the mock holds in EGP, even though HR-020 is marked fixed (#25).

## Top 10 to add (by value to an SMB owner)

1. **Offboarding with suite access revoked** (#11, #13, #12): one "employee leaves" flow that sets the last day and reason, collects equipment, closes SOS access in every app and hands off final pay. Today a delete leaves the person able to sign in. M.
2. **Leave policies with accruals, plus balances for employees** (#15, #16): policy templates, monthly accrual, carry-over and proration; employees see what they have left and can cancel. Leave is the most used HR feature. M + S.
3. **Employee self-service profile and directory** (#35, #2): employees see their record and request changes, and staff can find colleagues. This takes routine requests off HR. M + S.
4. **Bulk employee import** (#49): without it, a new customer types every employee into an 8-step wizard. M.
5. **Payment files that banks accept, and a currency per employee** (#24, #25): pain.001 for Swiss banks and WPS for UAE, behind a button, with each employee's salary currency used on every screen. M + S.
6. **Onboarding checklists in Tasks** (#10, #44): templates that create tasks for IT, the manager and the new hire on hire. M.
7. **Document expiry reminders** (#4, #3): track work permits, visas and IDs, and warn before they lapse; also add document categories and employee access. This is a compliance risk in the Middle East and Switzerland. M.
8. **Email notifications and reminders** (#40): pending approvals, probation or contract end and payslip ready reach people who don't keep HR open. M.
9. **From candidate to employee** (#31, #29): an offer letter, "Hire" that pre-fills the wizard, and a careers page from Website Manager. M + M.
10. **Light performance reviews linked to goals in Tasks** (#33, #34): a yearly or half-yearly review with self and manager forms. Owners expect it, and all three competitors offer it. L.
